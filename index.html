A Real Instrument

A Working Instrument, Engraved in Brass.

An astrolabe is the oldest computer built for the sky — a brass instrument astronomers and navigators turned by hand for over a thousand years to read the positions of the stars.

Turn it over. Watch the rete track the true positions of the planets at your exact moment of birth, doing real astronomical math the way the instrument always has.

Your Chart, Read Six Ways

Every House, Planet, and Angle — In Plain Language.

Love, Friends, Career, Home, Spirit, Purpose — laid out from your actual chart, not jargon. What's happening, and what to do about it.

Five are houses, read from your own rising sign, never the generic wheel. The sixth, Purpose, is different: it's the planet ruling your whole chart, not a sector of it — and usually the first reading to change if your birth time is off by a few minutes.

Most horoscopes default to your sun sign. This app centers your rising sign instead, on purpose — it's what actually sets your chart ruler. If the sign on your wheel isn't the one you expected, that's not a mistake. It's the more personal one.

One Reading, Not Four

Sun, Moon, Rising, and Ruler — Read as One Chart, Not Four.

Most horoscopes stop at your sun sign. This one reads your Sun, Moon, Rising, and the planet that rules your whole chart together, as a single throughline instead of four separate blurbs.

It closes by naming your chart's overall lean, so those four signs resolve into one picture instead of sitting side by side as trivia.

The Points Most Charts Skip

Every Point in Your Chart Gets Its Own Explanation.

Alongside the ten planets and four angles, this app plots the North Node, Chiron, Black Moon Lilith, and White Moon Selena — points most apps either omit or list without explaining. Each one gets the same full treatment as a planet: what it means, and what to do about it.

Astrologers pair Black Moon Lilith, the Moon's farthest swing from Earth, with White Moon Selena, its closest — and this is the first version of the app to track both by name.

The Sky, Pointing Back

Your Sign and the Real Sky Are Not the Same Thing.

Hold your phone up and the instrument follows the sky above you — your zodiac sign shown next to the actual constellation in that direction, because they are not the same thing, and this app is honest about the difference.
